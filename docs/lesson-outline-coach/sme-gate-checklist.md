# SME-gate checklist for teacher lesson outlines

This is the gate an instructional coach or department chair would apply before a lesson outline is taught or filed. SME here means that colleague: someone who knows the subject and how a class actually runs. It is not a medical, legal, or licensing review.

Use it on an outline a teacher already wrote. The coach marks each gate. The coach does not invent the missing lesson.

A gate is **met** only when another teacher in the same subject could teach that part tomorrow without guessing. Activity descriptions, topic names, and good intentions are not enough.

## Marks

Use these three marks and no others.

| Mark | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **met** | The outline states this, and a colleague could act on it without inventing the missing piece. |
| **partial** | Something is there, but a colleague would still have to guess. |
| **missing** | It is absent, or what is written is a topic or an activity rather than this criterion. |

Do not mark a gate `n/a`. If a gate barely constrains this outline, mark it **met** and say why in one sentence. Accessibility, timing, and risks are never optional.

## Verdict

Apply this after every gate is marked. Do not average them.

| Verdict | When |
|---|---|
| **ready** | Every gate is met. The outline can be taught as written. |
| **revise** | At least one gate is partial or missing, and the text is still a lesson a teacher can repair from the critique. |
| **blocked** | There is no outline to critique: a topic with no plan, a request to write the lesson from scratch, or text that is not safe to put in a public note (student names, grades, disability details, contact information, a channel name, a trip password, a token). |

**Ready** requires all twelve gates met. One missing objective is **revise**, not ready with a note.

## How to record a gate

In the critique note, one line per gate, in the order below. Name the evidence that is actually in the outline, or name what is absent. Do not supply the missing objective, quiz, or minute budget yourself and then mark the gate met.

```markdown
- **Objectives:** partial — students will "explore fractions," which names a topic, not an observable performance.
```

## The twelve gates

Copy this list into the critique and replace each box with the mark and one sentence.

- [ ] **Objectives** — observable student performance, not a topic or an activity.
- [ ] **Audience** — who the learners are, at the level a colleague needs.
- [ ] **Prerequisites** — knowledge, skill, and access the lesson assumes, and how the teacher will notice a gap.
- [ ] **Assessment alignment** — each objective has evidence that matches that objective.
- [ ] **Learning sequence** — opening, instruction, practice, and the student's own work, in an order that teaches the objective.
- [ ] **Timing** — a minute budget that fits the duration, including a cut line if time runs short.
- [ ] **Materials** — what the teacher and each learner need, and that it can be in the room.
- [ ] **Accessibility** — a learner who cannot use the planned sense, reading level, language, or device can still meet the objective.
- [ ] **Differentiation** — support for learners who are not ready, and an extension that does not replace the objective.
- [ ] **Checks for understanding** — at least one moment, before the end, when the teacher can see who is lost.
- [ ] **Closure** — learners use or restate the objective, and the outline says what comes next.
- [ ] **Risks and assumptions** — what must be true for the lesson to work, what the teacher will do if it is not, and how student work stays private.

## Gate criteria

### 1. Objectives

**Met when** the outline states what the student will do, under what conditions, well enough that two teachers would score the same work the same way. The verb is observable (`plot`, `explain`, `compare`, `solve`), not `understand`, `learn`, `explore`, or `be exposed to`. The number of objectives fits the duration: one clear performance is enough for a short period; a list that cannot be taught in the time is not met. If the outline names a standard or a course outcome, the objective matches that standard rather than a neighbor of it. If the setting requires standards and the outline names none, the gate is partial.

**Partial when** a performance is implied but a colleague would still choose the verb, the conditions, or which objective matters.

**Missing when** the outline only names a topic (`equivalent fractions`) or an activity (`watch the video`, `do the worksheet`) with no student performance.

### 2. Audience

**Met when** a colleague can tell who the lesson is for: grade or course level, the kind of group (whole class, small group, one learner), and the setting (in person, remote, hybrid). Approximate group size belongs here when it changes materials or timing. Do not require student names. A roster is a privacy problem, not a better audience statement.

**Partial when** only a grade is given and the setting or group shape would change the plan.

**Missing when** the outline could be for any learner.

### 3. Prerequisites

**Met when** the outline names the knowledge, skill, and access the lesson assumes (a prior concept, a vocabulary set, a login, a printed text, a physical skill) and how the teacher will notice if they are missing in the first few minutes. "They should know fractions" is partial. "Students can name a numerator and a denominator; the warm-up asks them to label both on one example" is met.

**Partial when** prerequisites are listed but the outline never checks them.

**Missing when** the lesson depends on prior learning or access and says nothing about it.

### 4. Assessment alignment

**Met when** each objective has a check that produces evidence of that objective. A number-line objective is checked by work on a number line, not by a vocabulary quiz about the word `equivalent`. The outline says what success looks like on that check (a correct placement, a written comparison, a solved problem with the shown method). Formative and summative checks both count. An exit ticket that measures a different skill is not alignment.

This is backward design: objective, then evidence, then the learning activity. An activity that is lively but proves a different goal leaves this gate partial or missing.

**Partial when** a check exists but it only samples some of the objectives, or the success criterion is "completion."

**Missing when** the outline never says how the teacher will know the objective was met.

### 5. Learning sequence

**Met when** the outline has an order a colleague can follow: how the lesson opens, how the new idea is taught, how students practice with support, and how students do the work themselves. The steps teach the stated objective. Students do the thinking, not only watch. Transitions that move the class from one step to the next are named when the plan would stall without them.

**Partial when** activities are listed but the order does not show how the objective is learned, or the teacher does all the work.

**Missing when** the outline is a pile of resources with no sequence.

### 6. Timing

**Met when** the outline states the total duration and a minute budget for each segment, and the parts add up. The budget includes transitions, handing out materials, and logging in. It includes a cut line: what to drop or shorten if the class is behind, without dropping the check that matches the objective.

**Partial when** a total duration is given but the segments are not budgeted, or the parts obviously overrun the period.

**Missing when** there is no duration.

### 7. Materials

**Met when** the outline lists what the teacher needs and what each learner needs (texts, slides, manipulatives, devices, accounts, copies). It says they are on hand, or what to prepare before the period. When a material is copyrighted, the outline says the teacher has the right to use it or that the material is teacher-made; do not paste a copyrighted worksheet into the note. A device lesson names what happens if the device is the material.

**Partial when** materials are named but not whether there are enough for the group, or a required login is assumed with no alternative.

**Missing when** a colleague would not know what to put on the desks.

### 8. Accessibility

**Met when** a learner who cannot see the planned visual, hear the planned audio, read at the outline's reading level, use the planned device, or work in the outline's only language can still meet the same objective. Name the concrete alternative: captions, a transcript, alt text or an oral description, a paper path, a glossary, extra time, or a response that is spoken instead of typed. "UDL" or "differentiated" with no alternative is not met. The objective stays the same; the path changes.

**Partial when** one barrier is handled and another barrier the lesson creates is not (a video has captions, but the only practice is a timed mouse game).

**Missing when** the lesson has a single path and no way through it for a learner the plan excludes.

### 9. Differentiation

**Met when** the outline gives a support for learners who do not yet have the prerequisite (a worked example, a smaller number set, a sentence frame) and an extension for learners who already do (a harder case of the same objective). The extension does not become a different lesson. The support does not remove the objective.

**Partial when** only one of those two exists, or the support replaces the objective with easier busywork.

**Missing when** every learner is assumed to need the same single path and gate 8's access alternatives are the only variation. Access and enrichment are different. Both belong in the outline.

### 10. Checks for understanding

**Met when** at least once before the close, the teacher sees individual evidence, not only a chorus of "yes" or a nod. A cold-call of one student is partial unless the outline also says how the teacher will see the others. Mini-whiteboards, a short written response, or circulating while students try one item are met when the outline says what the teacher is looking for.

This gate is the during-lesson check. Gate 4 is whether the evidence matches the objective. Both can fail on the same outline for different reasons.

**Partial when** the teacher asks the room and accepts a group answer.

**Missing when** the first evidence arrives at the end, or never.

### 11. Closure

**Met when** learners use or restate the objective in the last segment (they solve one more item, they state the rule in their own words, they compare two answers) and the outline says what the teacher will do with that, including what comes next period if this lesson sits in a sequence.

**Partial when** the lesson stops when time runs out, or the close is "any questions?"

**Missing when** there is no ending.

### 12. Risks and assumptions

**Met when** the outline states what must be true (the prior lesson happened, the devices are charged, the groups will form, the room has the materials) and what the teacher will do if it is not. If the lesson has physical risk, emotional risk, or a topic that can single a student out, the outline says how that is handled. If student work is collected, displayed, or sent to a tool outside the classroom, the outline says what is collected and that it is not a public note full of names.

If students use a generative tool, the outline says what the student must still produce and how the teacher will see the student's own reasoning. "They may use AI" with no product and no check is partial.

**Partial when** a real dependency is obvious from the rest of the outline and unstated, or a stated risk has no response.

**Missing when** the outline would collapse if one unstated assumption failed and it never says so. An outline with no physical, privacy, or technology risk can still be met: say that no such risk is apparent, and name the assumption that remains (for example "this follows yesterday's definition of a unit fraction").

## Before you mark ready

Read the outline once more against this list. Any one of these is at least **revise**:

- An objective that is only an activity or a topic.
- An assessment that scores a different skill than the objective.
- No audience.
- No duration, or a minute budget that cannot fit the period.
- A single path a learner cannot use, with no alternative that still meets the objective.
- An assumption that can sink the lesson (devices, prior knowledge, grouping, a login) with no response.
- Student names, grades, disability details, contact information, a channel name, or any secret. That outline is **blocked** until a version without them exists. Do not copy the private text into the critique to explain why.
